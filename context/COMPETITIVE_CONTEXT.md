# Competitive context

## What users do today instead

Conventional productivity trackers and todo apps, or spreadsheets, or nothing
at all. These tend to collapse a person into output metrics (tasks done,
streaks) and don't represent a whole life.

The closest real product is **Life Reset** (formerly Rise): 1.5M+ users, 4.7
stars, 76 countries, and its own App Store copy is "a self-improvement app
that turns your real goals into daily quests, XP, and streaks." Its top review
says it "finally makes self-improvement feel like a real-life RPG." The
concept is not our differentiator; the category is crowded and we are late to
it. What it does better: a bounded 66-day arc with a visible day counter, a
first-class streak, an onboarding that ends by showing you your program, and a
grid of days you can watch yourself move through. What it does that we decline:
seven bundled mini tools, a "Hard Mode" that punishes missed days, widgets,
push pressure, and a $12.99–$59.99 subscription ladder.

**Habitica** is the other reference: the original RPG habit tracker, heavy on
parties and guilds, aimed at people who want a game. **Notion and Obsidian
templates** are what the same person uses after abandoning apps. **A paper
journal** is the honest baseline: no retention mechanics, no cloud, and nobody
else can read it.

## Our wedge

A multidimensional character sheet and quest framing that rewards living —
relationships, recovery, adventure and joy are first-class stats — with
automated evidence but human-decided meaning: the product observes, the owner
decides what matters. Three things competitors cannot copy, because each cuts
against how they earn. Privacy is the product, not a bolt-on: one owner,
self-hosted, no telemetry, no external AI, no social layer. A missed day is
information, not a failure, so a deliberate rest day extends the streak and
there is no punishment mechanic. And the voice stays calm, not manipulative.
Retention-optimised apps cannot follow: streak anxiety is their retention
mechanic.

## Not competing on

- Breadth of integrations (deliberately earned slowly, not day one).
- Breadth of tools: no meditation, focus timer, calorie tracking, screen
  blocker or book summaries. A private ritual, not a suite.
- Being a multi-user product or company — this is personal software for now;
  see ROADMAP.md for when/whether that's reconsidered.
- Social features, leaderboards, or gamified competition with other people.
- Native mobile: a responsive web app, mobile-first, so no home-screen widgets
  and no push notifications.
- Price. There is no price and there are no other users.
